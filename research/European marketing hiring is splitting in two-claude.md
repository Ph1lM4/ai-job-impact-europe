# European marketing hiring is splitting in two — but not where you think

**The dominant narrative — that commercial marketing (PMM, growth, demand gen) is booming while brand/creative is dying — is partially right but requires significant correction.** Ravio's widely cited 34.7% hiring rate applies to **sales/commercial roles, not marketing** — marketing is tracked separately and is declining, especially at entry level (down ~76%). The real European bifurcation runs between **strategic marketing roles** (growing across both commercial and brand) and **execution-level roles** (declining across both), with AI accelerating the collapse of junior positions while senior strategic hiring remains robust. McKinsey's State of Marketing Europe 2026 confirms brand is actually the **#1 CMO priority** for 2026, complicating the "brand is dead" thesis. The data tells a more nuanced story than commonly assumed.

---

## The Ravio 34.7% figure needs reframing

The most-cited European hiring data point — Ravio's "commercial hiring at 34.7%, up 5.2%" — requires critical clarification. **In Ravio's taxonomy, "Commercial" means direct sales roles** (Account Executives, Sales Managers, Business Development), not a broader category inclusive of marketing. Marketing is tracked as an entirely separate function. This distinction matters enormously.

Ravio's 2026 Compensation Trends Report (published October 17, 2025; data from 1,500+ European tech companies, 300,000+ data points) shows that **Commercial/Sales is the only function with increased hiring** — every other function declined. Marketing specifically shows severe contraction at the entry level: **P1 (entry-level) marketing hiring dropped from 23.4% to 5.7%** — the lowest of any function and an approximately 76% year-over-year collapse. Meanwhile, marketing employee tenure rose **27.6%** (the largest increase of any function, now 2 years 10 months), suggesting companies are retaining experienced marketers but freezing new hiring.

Overall European tech hiring rates by country (all functions, Ravio October 2025):

| Country | 2025 Hiring Rate | YoY Change |
|---------|-----------------|------------|
| UK | 32% | -21% |
| Germany | 30% | +3% (only market growing) |
| Spain | 26% | -27% |
| Netherlands | 24% | -13% |
| France | 22% | -28% |
| Sweden | 17% | -34% |

**European tech hiring overall held at 29%**, stable from 2024 but down from 34% in 2023. Ravio does not publish marketing sub-role breakdowns (PMM, Growth, Demand Gen, etc.) publicly — that granular data sits behind their paid platform. What is publicly available confirms: **revenue-generating sales roles are booming; marketing is not expanding at the same rate.** [Ravio 2026 Compensation Trends Report, October 2025]

## McKinsey and Gartner reveal the brand vs. performance tension

### McKinsey State of Marketing Europe 2026

Published November 20, 2025, surveying **500 senior marketing decision-makers** across France, Germany, Italy, Spain, and the UK. Key hiring-relevant findings:

**Budgets are growing but teams are not.** Marketing budgets averaged **8.7% of company revenue** in 2025 across European companies. **72% of CMOs plan to increase budgets** relative to sales in 2026 (vs. 49% who actually did in the prior year — a gap worth noting). Only **5% of European marketing organizations decreased in size**, suggesting stability rather than growth.

**The 40.6% figure is widely misinterpreted.** It does not represent a 40.6% increase in upper-funnel budgets. It means **40.6% of surveyed European marketing leaders expect to increase the share of spend** allocated to upper-funnel (brand awareness) activities — the highest of any funnel stage. For comparison, 32.8% expect to increase mid-funnel share, and 33.4% lower-funnel share. Only 15.6% expect to decrease upper-funnel share (vs. 23.4% for lower funnel). The directional signal is clear: **brand/awareness is gaining wallet share from performance/conversion**, but the magnitude is a survey-response percentage, not a budget delta. [McKinsey, November 2025]

**Branding ranked #1 of 20 marketing priorities** for European CMOs. Gen AI ranked **#17** — what McKinsey called a "striking undervaluation." Only **6% of European marketing organizations** describe their gen AI use as mature; those few report **22% efficiency gains** with expectations to hit 28% within two years. This maturity gap suggests significant disruption ahead as the 94% playing catch-up adopt AI tools. [McKinsey, November 2025]

Budget allocation shifts (McKinsey citing Gartner data) show paid media's share growing from **27.9% to 30.6%**, while labor declined from **22.6% to 21.9%** and agencies from **21.6% to 20.7%**. The money is moving toward media and away from people.

**38% of European companies lack a dedicated marketing leader in the C-suite** — a structural gap that may be driving underinvestment in strategic marketing functions. [McKinsey, November 2025]

### Gartner CMO Spend Survey 2025

Published May 12, 2025 (402 CMOs, North America + UK + Europe combined — no European-only breakout available). No 2026 edition exists yet.

The Gartner data tells a different story from McKinsey on brand vs. performance. **54% of CMOs prioritize performance marketing** vs. just **22% who prioritize brand marketing** — a 2.5:1 ratio favoring performance. Brand awareness accounts for only **29% of media spend**. Yet **85% of CMOs agree** that investing in brand drives business results, and higher-performing businesses allocate more to brand. This gap between what CMOs believe and what they fund is the central tension in European marketing hiring.

On headcount, **39% of CMOs plan to reduce labor costs** through simplifying overlapping roles, reducing headcount, and centralizing capabilities. **22% said GenAI already enables them to reduce agency reliance** for creativity and strategy. Marketing budgets flatlined at **7.7% of revenue** (down from 11% pre-pandemic). [Gartner, May 2025]

## PMM demand is strong but not definitively the fastest-growing sub-role

Product Marketing Manager is consistently listed among the top in-demand marketing roles across European recruitment sources, but no single dataset confirms it as *the* fastest-growing marketing sub-role in Europe. The evidence:

**LinkedIn currently shows 4,000+ PMM jobs in the UK alone.** Go-to-Market Strategy — the core PMM competency — ranks as the **#8 fastest-growing marketing skill** on LinkedIn's 2026 Skills on the Rise report. Robert Half tracked **24,800 PMM job postings** in 2025, though this is US data. UK recruitment agencies (Intelligent People, 3Search, Flexa Careers) consistently list PMM alongside Growth Marketing Manager and Performance Marketing Specialist as top-demand roles. [LinkedIn, 2026; Robert Half, 2026; Intelligent People, 2025]

The Product Marketing Alliance's 2024/25 salary survey shows UK PMM median salaries at **£50,100–£52,900** (€57,600–€60,800), with Senior PMMs reaching **£78,400/€90,500** (European median) and Directors at **€102,700**. This positions PMM compensation **10–25% above** general Marketing Manager salaries, reflecting the strategic premium companies place on the role.

**Growth Marketing Manager** shows strong but moderate volume. LinkedIn lists **2,000+ Growth Marketing Manager jobs in the UK** currently. In the EEA, Growth PM roles (product management track) hover at only **20–52 open listings** at any time. The average remote Growth Marketing Manager salary in Europe is **€69,024** (RemoteRocketship, 1,080 postings tracked). UK salary range sits at **£50,000–£75,000** depending on seniority. [LinkedIn current; RemoteRocketship 2025; Intelligent People 2025]

Rory Woodbridge, a prominent European PMM consultant, notes that while "PMM is still on the rise in many parts of the world," the exponential growth phase may be ending: companies will "hire fewer product marketers, but hold them to a higher standard." [Product Marketer Substack, 2025 — opinion, not data]

## AI is reshaping execution roles, not eliminating strategy

The data on AI's impact is extensive and increasingly specific about which tasks face automation:

| Marketing Task | Automation Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Content creation (text) | 55% of marketers use AI; 86.4% use AI tools overall | RevvGrowth 2025; HubSpot 2026 |
| Email campaign management | 37% reduction in time | Kalungi/multiple, 2025 |
| Ad bidding/optimization | ~46% of advertisers use AI | eMarketer, cited by AI Digital |
| SEO keyword research | 58% of businesses use AI for topic research | Semrush 2025 |
| Landing page creation | 80% reduction in setup time | Kalungi 2025 |
| Campaign monitoring | 40% automation potential | McKinsey |

**McKinsey estimates up to 45% of marketing activities** could be automated with current technology. The Duke/Deloitte CMO Survey shows AI now powers **17.2% of all marketing activities** (doubled since 2022), projected to reach **44.2% within three years**. [McKinsey 2023; Duke/Deloitte CMO Survey 2025]

The headcount impact is concentrated at the junior level. A Stanford study found AI has caused a net loss of **~20% of headcount** in early-career (age 22–25) sales and marketing roles. Ravio's data confirms this: entry-level marketing hiring in European tech collapsed by 76%. Spencer Stuart's December 2025 survey found **36% of CMOs anticipate reducing headcount** within 12–24 months using AI; at large firms ($20B+ revenue), that rises to **47%**. Gartner reports **26% of CMOs planning headcount reductions specifically due to generative AI**. [Spencer Stuart via WSJ, December 2025; Gartner 2025; Stanford via Social Media Examiner 2025]

However, the ADWEEK/NewtonX March 2026 survey provides a counterpoint: "Most organizations report little to no AI-driven headcount reduction" yet, though day-to-day responsibilities have shifted with rising expectations around speed and output. **68% of organizations** say AI displaces tasks within roles, not entire roles (Morgan Stanley 2026 Enterprise AI Survey). The reality appears to be task displacement first, headcount reduction lagging. [ADWEEK March 2026; Morgan Stanley 2026]

**Roles most protected from AI:** PMM/positioning/GTM strategy, growth strategy, creative direction, brand strategy, customer insight/qualitative research. **Roles most exposed:** entry-level copywriting, social media scheduling, data entry/reporting, email execution, basic SEO tasks.

## The "full-stack marketer" is real but unmeasurable

The full-stack marketer trend is a genuine shift in hiring expectations but has **not crystallized into a standardized European job title** with trackable volume. No central dataset tracks this role specifically.

UK recruitment agency 3Search states directly: "General marketers will be in high demand this year. Employers are seeking candidates with broad skill sets." Career Group Companies notes that companies with "leaner hiring budgets" opt for broadly skilled marketers over specialists — suggesting the trend is **strongest at startups and SMEs**. The T-shaped marketer (deep expertise in one area, working knowledge across many) is the more dominant European framing than "full-stack." [3Search 2025; Career Group Companies 2025; WebProNews 2025]

In practice, the demand manifests not as "Full-Stack Marketer" job postings but as expanding requirements within existing titles — Marketing Managers expected to handle strategy, data analytics, SEO, content, paid media, CRM, and AI tools simultaneously. This is an expectations inflation story, not a new job-title story. Robert Half UK's top in-demand skills for 2026 confirm this: social media, graphic design, **data analysis, and generative AI use** are now expected alongside traditional marketing competencies. [Robert Half UK 2026]

## What the data actually shows about the commercial-vs-brand bifurcation

The user's hypothesis — that PMM/growth/demand gen (commercial) is growing while content/brand execution is flat or declining — is **partially validated but requires nuancing:**

**What the data supports:**
- Revenue-facing sales roles are the only function with growing hiring in European tech (Ravio: 34.7%, +5.2%). **Directly stated.**
- Performance marketing specialists are in "particularly strong demand" and increasing at the senior level (Marketing Week/Fortune Hill, late 2025). **Directly stated.**
- Entry-level marketing roles (including content execution) have collapsed by ~76% in European tech (Ravio). **Directly stated.**
- 39% of CMOs plan labor cuts via role simplification (Gartner). **Directly stated.**
- LinkedIn's fastest-growing marketing skills favor commercial/analytical capabilities: Performance Analysis (#1), Performance Marketing (#9), Go-to-Market Strategy (#8). **Directly stated.**

**What complicates the narrative:**
- **Brand is the #1 CMO priority** in Europe for 2026, not a declining investment area (McKinsey). The 40.6% upper-funnel directional signal is the strongest of any funnel stage. **Directly stated.**
- Only **5% of European marketing organizations** actually decreased in size (McKinsey). **Directly stated.**
- **85% of CMOs agree** brand investment drives results (Gartner). **Directly stated.**
- Brand strategy roles, employer brand specialists, and brand experience directors all appear on in-demand lists alongside growth and performance roles (Flexa Careers 2026). **Directly stated.**

**The more accurate framing:** The bifurcation runs between **strategic roles** (growing across both commercial and brand functions: PMM, growth strategy, brand strategy, marketing analytics) and **execution roles** (declining across both: junior copywriting, social media posting, email execution, basic reporting). AI is compressing the execution layer while companies invest more in senior strategic positions. The 2.5:1 Gartner ratio favoring performance over brand reflects current spend allocation, but the McKinsey data shows European leaders are actively rebalancing toward brand — meaning **brand strategy hiring may accelerate** even as brand execution roles shrink.

## Conclusion

Three findings stand out from the data. First, Ravio's 34.7% commercial hiring figure must be cited carefully — it refers exclusively to sales roles, and marketing is tracked separately with declining entry-level hiring (-76%). The European tech marketing story is not hiring growth but **team consolidation around senior, strategic talent**.

Second, the widely discussed upper-funnel budget shift is directionally real but smaller than headlines suggest. 40.6% of European CMOs plan to increase brand/awareness spend share (the highest of any funnel stage), signaling a genuine rebalancing from the performance-marketing default — but this is a survey-response percentage, not a budget multiplier.

Third, **the true European hiring bifurcation is vertical (strategic vs. execution), not horizontal (commercial vs. brand).** PMM, growth marketing, performance marketing, brand strategy, and marketing analytics roles are all in demand. What is declining is execution-layer work across all marketing domains, driven by AI adoption that is still in early stages (only 6% of European marketing organizations are AI-mature) but accelerating rapidly. The 94% of European marketing teams still catching up on AI maturity represent a coming wave of further role restructuring. Companies that have adopted AI report 22% efficiency gains today and expect 28% within two years — gains that will continue to reshape which marketing roles exist and which disappear.
