# Product Management Hiring in Europe 2025–2026

## Executive summary

European Product Management (PM) hiring has stabilized after the 2022–2023 downturn, but remains structurally tight and significantly more competitive than headline job counts suggest. Across the European Economic Area (EEA), open PM roles sit around 4,200 as of April 2025, down roughly 17% versus May 2024 but broadly flat over the prior six months, with the UK adding another ~1,200 openings. At the same time, European tech hiring overall is cautious: hiring rates across tech fell 15% year‑on‑year in 2023–2024 while attrition fell 33%, meaning companies are still net adding headcount but at lower velocity.[^1][^2]

The strongest PM demand concentrates in mid‑ and senior‑level roles; junior entry points remain structurally scarce (low single‑digit share of postings), and leadership roles are recovering but still a minority of openings. Remote PM work in continental Europe is rebounding faster than in the UK and US, with the EEA seeing a 111% increase in remote PM listings as a share of all PM jobs between late 2024 and early 2025, reaching around 28% of PM openings; in the UK, hybrid remains the dominant model at roughly half of listings.[^3][^4][^2][^1]

Across Europe, Product Management compensation continues to grow modestly (roughly 2–3% a year) and is now relatively uniform across major markets, with the UK leading in cash terms at both the P3 (mid‑level) and M3 (senior manager) levels. Sector‑wise, fintech, AI/ML‑driven software, SaaS infrastructure, and regulated digital services (payments, identity, regtech) are the most PM‑hungry segments, though product roles typically make up only 2–3% of total hiring even in product‑centric fintechs. Compared with the US, European PM hiring is on a similar upward trajectory but from a smaller base and with more conservative remote offerings and slower rebound in junior opportunities.[^5][^6][^7][^8][^9][^10][^11][^12][^1]


## 1. Open PM roles by country and YoY evolution

### 1.1 Available regional data and limitations

There is no single public dataset that provides a clean, PM‑only breakdown of open roles by individual European country with consistent methodology and year‑over‑year (YoY) comparisons. The most granular and methodologically documented European PM data currently available comes from James Gunaca’s monthly LinkedIn‑based PM job market tracking (used by Recruited.tech) and Lenny Rachitsky’s collaboration with TrueUp for global counts, but these are reported at regional (EEA, UK) rather than per‑country level. LinkedIn’s general jobs search surfaces raw counts such as “31k–36k Product Manager jobs in European Union”, but these include a mix of titles, duplicated postings, and broader geographies and are not directly comparable to curated datasets.[^13][^2][^10][^1]

Because of this, the only *confirmed* European PM vacancy figures that can be cited with methodology are for:

- EEA aggregate
- United Kingdom separately

For the other requested countries (Germany, France, Netherlands, Sweden, Spain, Switzerland, Austria), only indirect evidence exists (overall job vacancy rates, ICT specialist employment, sector‑specific PM hiring case studies). These can signal relative demand but not precise PM vacancy counts or YoY changes; any numeric extrapolation would be speculative and is therefore not provided.

### 1.2 Confirmed PM vacancy levels (EEA, UK)

Curated LinkedIn data reported by Recruited.tech and Product Sphere show the following snapshot as of early April 2025:[^1]

| Geography | Open PM roles (approx.) | Reference point | 6‑month change | YoY change vs ~May 2024 | Notes |
|-----------|-------------------------|-----------------|---------------|--------------------------|-------|
| EEA (excluding UK) | ~4,200 open PM roles | LinkedIn snapshot 7 April 2025 | −0.7% vs ~Oct 2024 | −17% vs May 2024 | Relatively stable last 6 months; modest cooling vs 2024 peak.[^1] |
| United Kingdom | ~1,200 open PM roles | LinkedIn snapshot 7 April 2025 | −8% vs ~Oct 2024 | −18% vs May 2024 | Sharper pullback than EEA; still one of largest PM markets globally.[^1] |

Later monthly reports show cyclical movements around this baseline: for example, UK PM jobs were down 4.9% in January 2025, then up 26% month‑on‑month in February 2025, illustrating short‑term volatility around a flat‑to‑slightly‑down annual trend. EEA PM jobs saw a January 2025 dip followed by a 15% rebound in February and a 5.6% increase in September 2025, again consistent with a choppy but broadly stable trend.[^14][^7][^15][^3]

### 1.3 Country‑level signals via general labour data (estimated, not PM‑specific)

To approximate relative tightness of PM hiring across specific European countries, general job‑vacancy and ICT‑specialist data can be used as proxies:

- Eurostat job vacancy rates for Q4 2024 show high overall vacancy rates in the Netherlands (4.1%), Austria (3.6%), and Belgium (4.1%), with lower rates in Spain (0.9%) and Switzerland (1.6–1.9%).[^16][^17]
- Separate analysis of total unfilled jobs across Europe finds that Germany (~1.05 million), the UK (~781,000), France (~504,000), and the Netherlands (~400,000) lead in absolute job vacancies, followed by Austria (~148,000), Spain (~145,000), Sweden (~113,000).[^18]
- Eurostat’s ICT‑specialist statistics show 10+ million ICT specialists in the EU in 2024 (5.0% of all employment), with particularly high ICT shares in Sweden (8.6%) and Luxembourg, and steady growth since 2014.[^19][^20][^21]

These data indicate that Germany, the UK, France, and the Netherlands host the largest pools of open knowledge‑worker roles broadly, with the Netherlands and Austria having particularly high vacancy *rates*, and Sweden and Switzerland particularly high ICT intensity. However, there is no PM‑specific breakdown, so any conversion from these vacancy totals to “open PM roles” by country would be speculative; such estimates are therefore omitted.[^20][^19][^16][^18]


## 2. Seniority distribution of PM roles

### 2.1 UK and EEA seniority mix (LinkedIn / Product Sphere)

Gunaca’s LinkedIn‑based analyses provide the clearest seniority breakdowns for PM roles in Europe.[^3][^1]

For the **UK**, January 2025 data show the following distribution of PM job listings by level:[^3]

| Level | Share of UK PM listings (Jan 2025) | Notes |
|-------|------------------------------------|-------|
| Associate / Junior PM | <2% | Confirms scarcity of true entry‑level PM roles; similar pattern across multiple months.[^3] |
| Product Manager (mid‑level) | 61% | Core of the market; most competitive level for candidates.[^3][^2] |
| Senior Product Manager | 21% | Strong demand for experienced ICs; senior PM job count grew even when total jobs declined.[^1][^3] |
| Product Leadership (Head, Director, VP, CPO) | ~17% | Leadership share has shown the most resilient YoY growth globally.[^3][^2] |

For the **EEA (excluding UK)**, Recruited’s April 2025 article summarises LinkedIn data as:[^1]

- Around **72%** of listings tagged as standard “Product Manager” (mid‑level).
- About **16%** as “Senior Product Manager”.
- Roughly **3%** as junior/associate roles.
- The remainder (~9%) in leadership positions (Head of Product, Director, VP, CPO).

These distributions imply that **80–90% of European PM hiring is for mid‑ to senior‑level IC roles**, with leadership representing under one‑fifth of postings and junior roles in the very low single digits.[^2][^1][^3]

### 2.2 Global context from Lenny / TrueUp and Live Data (US‑heavy)

Globally, Lenny Rachitsky’s analyses combining TrueUp’s job‑posting data and Live Data Technologies’ job‑change data for the US confirm a strong tilt toward seniority: leadership PM roles grew 31% year‑over‑year in 2025, outpacing senior and mid‑level PM roles, while junior roles remained a small minority of openings. Remote PM roles globally are down ~35% from peak and continue to shrink, with more hiring shifting back to on‑site or hybrid, especially in the US.[^11][^22][^2]

These global figures are not directly transferrable to Europe, but they are directionally consistent with the European seniority split observed in LinkedIn’s UK/EEA data.


## 3. AI Product Manager roles in Europe

### 3.1 Confirmed AI PM vacancy counts (EEA, UK)

Recruited’s April 2025 European PM job‑market article is one of the few sources to explicitly count AI‑focused PM titles in Europe.[^1]

According to LinkedIn job‑title filters as of early April 2025:[^1]

- **United Kingdom:** open **AI Product Manager** listings fluctuate between **11 and 16** at any given time.
- **EEA (excluding UK):** open AI PM listings range between **30 and 40**.

In both regions, AI PM roles remain a **very small fraction** of all PM postings; Recruited notes that neither AI PM nor Growth PM titles have yet achieved breakout scale, especially compared with Technical PM roles, which account for 1–5% of PM jobs in Europe.[^1]

Combining UK and EEA figures implies on the order of **40–60 open AI PM roles across Europe** at any given time in early 2025, concentrated in larger tech hubs. This is orders of magnitude smaller than the global total: by mid‑2025, TrueUp data showed roughly **688 open AI PM roles globally** (spanning AI companies plus AI‑focused PM roles in broader tech firms).[^23][^1]

### 3.2 Growth trajectory

While absolute numbers are small, several indicators suggest **fast relative growth** in European AI‑centric PM work:

- LinkedIn’s “Jobs on the Rise 2026” for Europe highlights AI‑related roles (e.g., AI engineer, AI consultant) among the fastest‑growing titles, with significant concentration in Germany and the UK; many of these roles sit adjacent to or overlap with product responsibilities.[^24]
- PCN’s 2026 fintech product‑hiring report finds that **94% of analyzed fintech product roles include AI‑related signals** (e.g., ML, LLMs, model governance) even when the job title is not explicitly “AI Product Manager”, showing that AI skill demand is diffusing through mainstream PM roles.[^9]
- Sector reports in fintech and SaaS repeatedly describe demand for “technically fluent PMs” and “product‑led engineers” who can work closely with AI platforms, rather than net‑new AI PM headcount, supporting the view that AI is reshaping PM skill requirements more than the absolute number of PM seats (so far).[^6][^25][^9]

Given the small base and limited historical data, a reliable quantitative growth rate (for example, compound annual growth) for *European* AI PM titles cannot be estimated from public sources; only global growth rates (e.g., TrueUp’s AI PM series) are currently well quantified.[^23]


## 4. Salary benchmarks by country and seniority (P3 and M3)

### 4.1 Ravio benchmark definitions

Ravio’s compensation benchmarks, based on data from over 1,000 European tech companies and 300,000+ datapoints, define two reference PM levels:[^1]

- **P3 Product Manager (mid‑level IC):** an established individual contributor with deep knowledge of their discipline and moderate impact on discipline success.
- **M3 Product Manager (Senior Manager):** a leader managing other managers and accountable for team‑level outcomes.

Values below are median base salaries in GBP (with indicative euro equivalents where provided), for 2023–2024 data published in the 2025 Compensation Trends report, and for 2025 data in Ravio’s October/November 2025 blog updates.[^8][^5][^1]

By funding stage, Ravio finds that late‑stage tech companies pay roughly **13% more** for P3 PMs and **32% more** for M3 PMs than early‑stage startups, with growth‑stage companies having recently cut M3 PM salaries most aggressively. Overall PM salary growth across Europe is modest but positive, with P3 and M3 medians typically rising 2–3% annually in 2025, slower than 2021–2022 boom years but roughly in line with cooling inflation.[^5][^8][^1]

### 4.4 Cross‑checking with crowd‑sourced salary data (Glassdoor / Mind the Product)

Crowd‑sourced salary data provide a useful but noisier cross‑check:

- Glassdoor‑based analysis for 2024 highlights average PM salaries around **£60.8k in the UK**, **~£58k in Germany**, and **significantly higher in Switzerland (~£94k)**, broadly consistent with Ravio’s relative ranking (UK and Switzerland highest, Spain and some Southern/Eastern markets lower).[^26]
- European Transparent IT Job Market Report 2025 confirms that **Switzerland remains the highest‑paying IT market in Europe**, followed by Western/Nordic countries, reinforcing the expectation that Swiss PM salaries likely exceed Ravio’s top benchmarks, although role‑level medians are not publicly broken out.[^27]

These sources lack clean seniority splits comparable to P3/M3, so they are best viewed as directional validation rather than substitutes for Ravio’s levelled benchmarks.


## 5. Hiring rate vs. attrition rate for PMs

### 5.1 Tech‑wide European hiring and attrition (Ravio)

Ravio’s 2025 Compensation Trends report provides the most robust aggregate view of European tech hiring and attrition, though it is **not PM‑specific**:[^1]

- **Hiring rates** across European tech companies fell from **34% to 29%** year‑on‑year (2023–2024), a drop of **15%** in relative terms.
- **Attrition rates** fell more sharply, by **33%** year‑on‑year, reflecting employees’ increased reluctance to leave stable roles in a cautious market.
- 65% of surveyed companies plan to **grow headcount** over the next 12 months, up from ~45% in the prior year, while only 3% plan explicit headcount reductions via non‑hiring strategies.[^1]

These data indicate that, across European tech as a whole, **companies are net adding headcount**, but at a slower pace than during the 2020–2022 boom, with lower churn and more selective hiring.[^1]

### 5.2 PM‑specific implications and known gaps

No public dataset currently quantifies **European PM‑specific hiring versus attrition** (hires and departures) comparable to Live Data Technologies’ US‑focused job‑change data used in Lenny’s 2024–2025 analyses. However, several PM‑focused indicators suggest that PM headcount in Europe is at least stable to modestly growing:[^11]

- PM job postings in the EEA and UK have stabilized after 2023’s declines, with multiple months of mid‑single‑digit percentage growth (e.g., EEA +5.6% and UK +5.0% in September 2025, UK +21% and EEA +10% in February 2026), indicating rising hiring demand.[^7][^29][^30]
- Global PM reports consistently highlight **increasing leadership PM roles** (globally +31% YoY in 2025), even in months when total PM roles decline, suggesting continued investment in senior product talent rather than net headcount cuts.[^2]
- European fintech product‑hiring studies show product roles accounting for 2% of total hiring but remaining central to strategic capability building, with emphasis on targeted senior IC and leadership roles over broad junior hiring.[^12][^6][^9]

Given the absence of European PM‑specific churn data, it is not possible to state a numeric **net PM headcount growth rate** for Europe. The best evidence indicates that PM headcount is **not shrinking** and is likely gradually increasing, but with companies favoring **selective senior hires and internal mobility** over large net-new additions.


## 6. Remote vs. hybrid vs. on‑site PM roles by country

### 6.1 PM‑specific work‑environment mix (UK vs EEA)

Gunaca’s LinkedIn‑based PM reports and Recruited’s April 2025 article provide PM‑specific work‑environment splits for the UK and EEA.[^3][^1]

For the **United Kingdom** (PM roles only):[^3][^1]

- Hybrid is the dominant mode, accounting for **around 44–50%** of PM listings.
- On‑site roles make up **about one‑third** of PM jobs.
- Remote‑only roles comprise **roughly 18%** of PM listings.
- Over the six months to April 2025, the share of remote PM roles grew by about **9%**, driven by an **18% drop in on‑site** postings and a **7% decline in hybrid**, suggesting a modest shift back towards remote flexibility from a relatively low base.[^1]

For the **EEA (excluding UK)** (PM roles):[^1]

- Remote roles now make up **around 28%** of all PM jobs, the highest proportion since tracking began in mid‑2024.
- Since October 2024, the share of remote PM listings has increased by **111%**, largely at the expense of hybrid roles (−17%), while on‑site roles declined modestly (−5%).
- This makes the EEA the **leading global region for remote PM roles** as a share of total PM jobs in Gunaca’s dataset.[^1]

### 6.2 Broader remote/hybrid trends for all roles (LinkedIn Economic Graph)

LinkedIn’s 2024 Global State of Remote and Hybrid Work report, which covers all job families, offers context for major European countries:[^4]

- In July 2024, hybrid postings accounted for **38.3% of UK job ads**, with **10.3% remote**, confirming the dominance of hybrid but with a non‑trivial remote segment.
- France showed a higher prevalence of on‑site hiring across company sizes, with hybrid around **29.7%** of postings and remote substantially lower, indicating a more office‑centric culture than Germany or the UK.[^4]
- Germany saw **growth in remote hires** for small and medium‑sized firms (remote share up 1.4–6.8 percentage points YoY), even as large firms modestly reduced remote hiring, suggesting sustained demand for remote knowledge workers including PMs in flexible SMEs.[^4]

These data, though not PM‑specific, align with the PM‑only view: **continental Europe, particularly the EEA, is relatively more remote‑friendly for PMs than the UK**, where hybrid is still dominant and remote has recovered only gradually.


## 7. Industries hiring PMs most aggressively in Europe

### 7.1 Fintech and payments

Multiple sectoral reports identify fintech and digital financial services as one of the **most PM‑intensive hiring segments** in Europe:

- PCN’s 2026 fintech product‑hiring analysis across 25 European fintech and payments firms (≈2,000 product professionals) found that **product roles accounted for just over 2% of total hiring**, with hiring skewed strongly toward execution‑focused ICs (PMs and Senior PMs) rather than leadership expansion.[^6][^9]
- Nearly **one‑third of EU fintech product hiring is driven by just two companies, Wise and Revolut**, underscoring the concentration of demand in a few large-scale players.[^9]
- Fintech hubs such as **London, the Dutch Randstad (Amsterdam/Rotterdam/Utrecht), and Berlin** host the largest absolute numbers of product professionals (e.g., 444 PMs in London‑based fintechs, 286 in Randstad, 111 in Berlin in the PCN sample), and continue to add targeted PM headcount for platform, compliance, and embedded‑finance initiatives.[^31][^9]

### 7.2 AI, data, and infrastructure software

Across broader tech, AI, cloud, and cybersecurity are leading growth areas for knowledge workers, and they rely heavily on product roles:[^32]

- Eurostat and independent analyses of Europe’s tech labour market note strong demand for **AI, cloud infrastructure, and cybersecurity roles**, with over 10 million ICT specialists employed in 2024 and a policy target of 20 million digital experts by 2030.[^21][^19][^32]
- PCN and EC1 Partners reports emphasize **AI‑enabled SaaS, regtech, and data‑platform companies** hiring product talent selectively to build capabilities around LLMs, risk/compliance automation, and cross‑border payments platforms.[^25][^9]
- Lenny’s global AI PM analysis shows AI PM roles as one of the fastest‑growing PM specializations worldwide, even if European AI PM titles remain relatively few in absolute number.[^23]

### 7.3 Marketplaces, B2B SaaS, and regulated digital services

Other European segments with sustained PM hiring include:

- **Marketplaces and platforms** (e.g., mobility, logistics, talent marketplaces) where PMs are needed to balance multi‑sided incentives; while no quantitative European PM‑only counts are available, sector hiring reports consistently list PM among core roles.[^32]
- **Digital health and healthtech**, where PMs help productize clinical workflows; job boards and VC portfolios show a steady stream of senior PM roles in European healthtech companies, but without aggregated statistics.[^33][^34]
- **Identity, regtech, and digital public‑sector platforms**, where PMs with regulatory fluency are sought to build digital identity, e‑KYC, and credential infrastructures across the EU and UK, as evidenced by repeated senior PM mandates in these niches.[^35][^25][^6]

Overall, the **most consistently PM‑hungry European industries** are fintech/payments, AI‑enabled B2B SaaS and data platforms, and regulated digital services (identity, regtech, health), with marketplaces a secondary but still active area.


## 8. Europe vs. US PM hiring trajectory

### 8.1 Global PM openings and growth (TrueUp / Lenny)

Lenny Rachitsky’s March 2026 State of the Product Job Market, based on TrueUp’s tracking of PM roles at 9,000+ tech companies, provides the clearest global baseline:[^10]

- As of early 2026 there are **over 7,300 open PM roles globally at tech companies**, the highest level in more than three years.
- This is **75% above the early‑2023 low** and **nearly 20% above** the start of 2026, confirming a strong global recovery in PM demand.[^36][^10]

Earlier 2025 analysis from the same source reported **over 6,000 open PM roles globally**, already 53.6% above the 2023 bottom and 11% up year‑to‑date at that time.[^23]

### 8.2 Regional dynamics: EEA/UK vs. US

Gunaca’s monthly PM jobs reports, based on LinkedIn, give comparative regional growth patterns:[^37][^7][^2]

- In **May 2025**, EEA and US both led month‑over‑month PM job growth at around **+5–7%**, with the US adding the largest absolute number of new listings (+408 net roles), but from a much larger base.[^38]
- In **September and October 2025**, **EEA PM roles grew 5.6–7.0%**, while the **US was roughly flat to slightly negative (−0.1% to −0.2%)**, implying that Europe led the late‑2025 PM hiring upswing on a percentage basis even though the US still dominated absolute job counts.[^7][^37]
- In **July 2025**, a global PM jobs report highlighted a steep **23% month‑on‑month drop in European PM postings**, compared with a more modest **6% decline in the US**, underscoring higher short‑term volatility in European PM hiring.[^39]
- By **February 2026**, global PM roles were up **12% month‑on‑month**, with the **UK leading regional growth at +21%**, the **EEA at +10%**, and the **US at +15%**, indicating synchronized expansion across major regions into early 2026.[^29][^30]

Overall, this suggests:

- The **US still accounts for the largest share** of open PM roles globally and often leads in absolute net gains in openings.[^10][^38]
- **Europe (EEA+UK) has seen comparable or stronger *percentage* growth** in several 2025–2026 periods, especially in late 2025 and early 2026, despite starting from a smaller base.[^29][^37][^7]
- Remote PM roles are recovering faster in continental Europe than in the US, where remote PM roles remain ~35% below peak and continue to decline.[^11][^4][^1]

### 8.3 How Europe compares to Lenny’s global findings

Relative to Lenny’s global baseline (7,300+ open PM roles globally, +75% from 2023 low, +20% YTD 2026), Europe’s position can be summarized as follows:[^36][^10][^1]

- Europe accounts for **a minority but material share** of global PM openings: EEA (~4,200) plus UK (~1,200) together represent roughly **70–80% of the curated TrueUp/LinkedIn PM openings that are explicitly located in Europe**, excluding Europe‑based roles at non‑European firms not counted in the EEA/UK regional tallies.[^10][^1]
- European PM hiring has **largely mirrored the global recovery** from the 2023 trough, with strong leadership growth, steady senior PM demand, and a slow rebound in junior roles, but with **greater volatility and more conservative remote offerings in the UK** compared with both the EEA and the US.[^7][^2][^3][^1]
- AI‑related PM demand is growing globally, but European AI PM titles are still a **small subset** of the global 600–700 AI PM roles tracked by TrueUp in 2025, with only 40–60 AI PM openings in Europe at any given time in early 2025.[^23][^1]


## 9. Where data is missing or only approximate

For transparency, several of the user‑requested metrics lack robust, Europe‑specific PM data:

- **Open PM roles by each listed country (UK, Germany, France, Netherlands, Sweden, Spain, Switzerland, Austria)**: only UK and EEA aggregates are available with PM‑specific counts and YoY changes. General job‑vacancy and ICT‑specialist statistics exist by country but are not disaggregated to the PM occupation and are therefore only directional proxies.[^19][^21][^16][^18][^1]
- **PM‑specific hiring vs. attrition rates in Europe**: Ravio reports tech‑wide hiring and attrition rates, and Live Data Technologies provides US‑focused PM churn data, but no European PM‑only hire/exit series is publicly available.[^11][^1]
- **Precise remote vs. hybrid vs. on‑site splits by individual European country for PMs**: only the UK and EEA receive PM‑specific splits; other countries are covered only by all‑job remote/hybrid statistics.[^4][^1]
- **Sector‑specific PM vacancy counts (e.g., exact number of fintech vs. health vs. marketplace PM roles)**: available reports quantify relative shares (for example, product roles ≈2% of hiring in leading fintechs) and qualitative demand patterns but not absolute PM job counts by sector across Europe.[^12][^6][^9]

Where such gaps exist, this report refrains from extrapolating European PM figures from US or global datasets and instead confines itself to **confirmed counts, clearly labelled proxies, and directional interpretation**.
