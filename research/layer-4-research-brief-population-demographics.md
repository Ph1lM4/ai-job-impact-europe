You are a demographer and labour market economist. Research European population dynamics at multiple time horizons — recent history (1 year, 5 years), medium-term (to 2030), and long-term (to 2040, 2050) — to quantify the workforce supply context into which AI disruption is arriving.

### B.2 Working-age population projections

## PART D — CONNECTING DEMOGRAPHICS TO THE AI STORY

This section bridges Layer 4 to the other layers. The research should produce clear answers to:

### D.1 The substitution question

For each major occupation group on the AI exposure map (Layer 1):

- How many workers in that group will retire by 2030? By 2040?
- Can AI plausibly fill the gap left by retirement, or is the work inherently physical/relational?
- Where retirement + AI displacement compound (both forces shrinking the workforce in the same occupation), what's the net effect?

### D.2 The mismatch question

- AI displaces workers from Zone A (clerical/cognitive) occupations
- Demographics create shortages in Zone C (physical/care/trade) occupations
- How portable are skills between these zones? What does retraining actually cost and how long does it take?
- Historical parallels: when farm workers were displaced by mechanisation (1900–1960), they moved to manufacturing. When manufacturing workers were displaced by automation (1970–2000), they moved to services. Where do displaced knowledge workers move?

### D.3 The immigration question

- If Europe needs ~1M additional workers per year just to maintain current workforce levels, and AI simultaneously displaces millions of knowledge workers — does AI reduce or increase the need for immigration?
- The skill composition mismatch: immigration tends to supply lower-skill workers; AI tends to displace middle-skill workers; shortages tend to be in both low-skill (care, agriculture) and high-skill (engineering, IT, healthcare) occupations
- Political feasibility: can European countries sustain the immigration levels needed to offset demographic decline?

### D.4 The generational question

- Boomers (1946–1964): retiring now through ~2030. The wave of institutional knowledge leaving organisations. AI as a potential "knowledge capture" tool.
- Gen X (1965–1980): in senior roles, facing the "too old to retrain, too young to retire" squeeze if AI displaces their function
- Millennials (1981–1996): the generation most exposed to AI-driven job transformation — they're mid-career and predominantly in Zone B occupations
- Gen Z (1997–2012): entering a labour market that's simultaneously shrinking (demographics) and transforming (AI). First generation to be AI-native — advantage or vulnerability?
