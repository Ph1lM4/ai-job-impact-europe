# The state of tech hiring across every role, 2022–2026

**Tech hiring has clawed back roughly half of what the industry lost since the April 2022 peak — and the recovery is accelerating in early 2026, but the gains are distributed unevenly across roles in ways that reshape how every startup should think about building teams.** Open listings at tech companies globally now stand at roughly **265,000** (TrueUp, March 2026), up ~44% from the early-2023 trough of ~163,000 but still ~45% below the all-time peak of ~478,000. The story beneath the headline number matters far more: AI/ML roles are hockey-sticking, recruiter demand is flashing a bullish leading indicator, design has flatlined, and scrum master is the one role actually shrinking. What follows is a role-by-role breakdown mirroring the analytical framework Lenny Rachitsky has built across four editions of his "State of the Product Job Market" reports, extended across all ten classic startup functions.

---

## Product management is back — and AI PMs are the fastest-growing subspecialty

Lenny's March 2026 data (his fourth and "most optimistic" edition) shows **7,300+ open PM roles at tech companies globally**, the highest count since 2022. That figure is **75% above the early-2023 trough** and already **up ~20% since January 2026**. TrueUp's year-over-year metric shows PM openings up **27.2%**. The trajectory is clear: after a brutal 2023 when open PM roles bottomed near 4,000, the recovery has been steady and is now accelerating.

The composition of PM hiring has shifted decisively. **AI PM roles are exploding** — Lenny counted 688 open AI PM positions in May 2025, and by early 2026 described demand as "similarly exploding" alongside AI engineering roles. These span two categories: PM roles at AI-native companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor, Lovable) and AI-specific PM roles at non-AI companies (e.g., AI PM at Figma). AI PMs in the U.S. average **$133,600** annually, climbing to **$200,000** for senior positions. The top three hard skills now listed in PM job postings are SQL, Jira, and experience with LLMs — a striking shift from even two years ago.

The PM-to-engineer ratio is inverting. Andrew Ng told Lenny's Podcast that one of his teams proposed staffing a project with a **1:0.5 PM-to-engineer ratio** — more PMs than engineers — because AI has made engineering execution so much faster that deciding *what* to build has become the bottleneck. This is an extreme case, but Etsy's CPO reported their ratio shifting from 1:10 to 1:6, and the directional trend is consistent. Companies are hiring **fewer but more senior PMs**, with a pronounced shift toward Lead/Senior++ roles and a corresponding decline in Entry/Mid-level PM share. Junior PM roles at startups declined **58%** in recent data.

The Bay Area's gravitational pull on PM roles keeps strengthening: **23% of all open PM roles globally** are now Bay Area–based, up **50% since 2022**. Berlin and Austin have entered the top 10 PM cities, displacing Boston and LA. Remote PM positions have dropped from 35% to roughly 22.5% of open roles — a ~35% decline. Salary direction is flat for traditional PMs but rising for AI PMs, with Group PM new-offer compensation up **+25%** from 2023 to 2025.

## Engineering demand is at post-2022 highs, but the market has split in two

The headline: **67,000+ open engineering roles at tech companies globally, with 26,000 in the U.S. alone** (March 2026). TrueUp shows software engineering openings up **34.1% year-over-year**, with growth accelerating since January 2026. These are the most open engineering roles since late 2022. Lenny frames this plainly: "AI has NOT slowed demand for software engineers (yet)."

But the market has bifurcated dramatically. **AI/ML engineering is the single fastest-growing job category in tech.** TrueUp's "Data Science & Machine Learning" category shows **9,075 open roles, up 90.1% year-over-year** — the highest growth rate of any tracked category. ML Engineer was the **#1 growing job title in 2025 (+40% YoY)** according to Bloomberry's analysis of 180 million job postings, on top of +78% in 2024. Robert Half counted **49,200 AI/ML/data science postings in the U.S. in 2025, up 163% from 2024**. Roughly **one-third of all open AI roles globally** are concentrated in the Bay Area — more than the next nine locations combined.

The flip side is a collapse in entry-level engineering hiring. Ravio's European tech data shows a **73% decrease in P1/P2 (entry-level) hiring rates** in 2025. Big Tech has hired **50% fewer fresh graduates** over the past three years. A Harvard study of 62 million workers found that when companies adopt generative AI, **junior developer employment drops ~9–10% within six quarters** while senior employment barely budges. This is the defining structural shift: companies want fewer, more senior engineers who can leverage AI tools to ship faster. AI-authored code now accounts for **26.9% of production code** at companies tracked by GitHub, and 93% of developers use an AI coding assistant at least monthly.

Within engineering subcategories, **frontend roles have declined the most**, likely due to "vibe-coding" tools like Replit, Lovable, and Bolt.new that can generate UI code directly. **DevOps roles are also down 4%** year-over-year on TrueUp. Platform/infrastructure and backend roles remain strong, driven by the compute and data infrastructure buildout required by AI. Median AI engineer salary has jumped to **$206,000** in 2025 — a $50,000 increase from the prior year — while general senior software developer compensation has actually declined ~10% in some datasets.

## Design has flatlined — and Lenny thinks AI is the reason

This is the most sobering trend in the data. **Open design roles have been relatively flat since early 2023**, sitting at roughly **5,700 globally** as of March 2026. Unlike PM and engineering — which bottomed in 2023 and have since recovered 75% and 34%+ respectively — design never bounced back. Lenny's analysis is direct: "Unlike PM and eng, which started growing in 2024 (two years post-ChatGPT), design didn't."

The PM-to-designer ratio has flipped. In mid-2023, there were more open designer roles than PM roles. Since then, PM demand has pulled away, and the ratio now stands at **1.27x more open PM roles than design roles**. Lenny's theory: "Because AI is allowing engineers to move so quickly, there's less opportunity — and less desire — to involve the traditional design process." When an engineer can ship a working prototype in hours using AI coding tools, the sequential design → spec → build workflow compresses, and design's role in the production pipeline shrinks.

The picture is more nuanced than pure decline. Figma's 2026 survey found **40% of hiring managers plan to open more design headcount** in coming months, and **73% now require candidates to be proficient in AI tools**. Designer Fund estimates design postings across its portfolio were **up ~60% in 2025 versus 2024**. The demand shift is toward senior, strategic designers — **56% of hiring managers want more senior design hires** versus just 25% for junior roles. The NNGroup calls the UX job market "stabilizing, but still competitive." The painful truth is that AI hasn't eliminated design work — it has commoditized output (wireframes, layouts, UI variations) while making judgment, systems thinking, and strategic design sense more valuable. Designers who can design *for* AI experiences and *with* AI tools will thrive; those who produce pixel-level artifacts may not.

## Growth remains the breakout product-adjacent role

Lenny's July 2024 collaboration with Live Data Technologies surfaced a finding he didn't expect: **Growth was the #1 fastest-growing product-adjacent role**, outpacing even engineering. "I didn't see this coming," Lenny wrote. "I've always thought of growth roles as a niche, and not one that was significantly expanding. I was wrong." The data showed roughly **80,000 active growth roles in the U.S.**, with **1,000–2,000 growth hires per month**. Top hirers included HubSpot, Salesforce, TikTok, Amazon, Uber, Atlassian, Google, Adobe, and Walmart.

TrueUp's March 2026 data shows the combined "Marketing, Growth & Community" category at **11,156 open roles, up 26.9% year-over-year**. Several forces sustain growth role demand into 2026: rising customer acquisition costs push companies toward product-led growth strategies; AI tools enable smaller growth teams to run more experiments faster; the post-2023 emphasis on capital efficiency metrics (CAC payback, Magic Number, net revenue retention) favors data-driven growth practitioners over brand spending; and the convergence of product, marketing, and analytics continues creating cross-functional growth roles that didn't exist a decade ago.

Growth roles are being **augmented rather than replaced by AI**. Tools like Amplitude, Mixpanel, and Mutiny let growth PMs and growth marketers automate A/B testing, run personalized lifecycle campaigns at scale, and deploy predictive analytics — amplifying the impact of each hire rather than eliminating positions. Average U.S. Growth PM total compensation sits at approximately **$137,600**.

## Sales is restructuring around AI — SDRs shrink while sales engineering surges

The sales function shows the clearest internal divergence of any category. TrueUp tracks **20,151 open sales roles (+30.5% YoY)** and **6,231 open sales engineering roles (+66.7% YoY)** — making sales engineering one of the fastest-growing subcategories across all of tech. Customer success adds another **9,156 openings (+33.7% YoY)**.

The SDR/BDR story is the most dramatic. An Emergence Capital survey of 560+ B2B SaaS companies found that **36% decreased SDR/BDR headcount** in the past year — the highest cut rate of any sales function — while only **19% increased SDR teams**, the lowest growth rate. Companies are deploying AI SDR platforms (Artisan, 11x.ai, Apollo, Outreach) that handle research, personalization, and follow-up sequencing at a fraction of the cost: roughly **$1,000/month versus $55,000+/year** for a human SDR. These tools claim **4–7x higher conversion rates** and up to **70% cost reduction**. Jason Lemkin revealed on Lenny's podcast that SaaStr went from 8–9 human salespeople to **1.2 humans + 20 AI agents** with equivalent business performance.

But fully autonomous AI SDRs have largely failed at scale. Companies that deployed them as complete replacements "have largely reverted to hybrid models or returned to human-first approaches." Bain Capital Ventures research found cold calls still outperform AI-generated email outreach, partly because AI-generated outbound has created an "email slop" problem that tanks response rates. The emerging model is **fewer, more skilled SDRs augmented by AI** — handling strategic outreach and relationship selling while AI manages volume.

**Sales engineering stands apart as the most resilient sales function**: only 14% of companies decreased SE headcount, the smallest decrease of any sales role. Revenue Operations has seen **explosive growth** — 79% of organizations now have a formal RevOps function (up from ~30% in 2020), and Gartner predicts 75% of the highest-growth companies will adopt RevOps by 2026. An entirely new role, **GTM Engineer**, has emerged with hiring doubling year-over-year for two consecutive years and salaries reaching **$180K–$240K base** at AI companies.

## Operations faces the deepest AI-driven compression

Business operations and program management shows a paradox in the TrueUp data: **16,555 open roles, up 43.6% year-over-year** — seemingly healthy. But Ravio's European tech data tells the structural story: **operations hiring rates declined 20% in 2025** to 27%, with the **lowest salary increase eligibility** (14% of employees received raises versus 23% overall) and the **lowest promotion rates** (3.4%) of any function. Administrative role hiring is **down 32.5% globally**, and 50% of compensation leaders explicitly cited AI automation as the reason for deprioritizing these positions.

The bifurcation is sharp. Routine operational work — scheduling, reporting, basic coordination, document summarization — is being automated aggressively. Gartner predicts that by 2026, **20% of organizations will use AI to flatten org structures**, eliminating more than half of current middle management positions. But strategic operations work — AI implementation, systems design, cross-functional alignment — is becoming more important, not less. Business development and partnerships shows **4,296 open roles, up 34.9% YoY** on TrueUp, suggesting the strategic end of operations is thriving even as the administrative end contracts.

## Marketing splits between AI-resilient strategy and AI-vulnerable execution

The marketing function mirrors the broader pattern: **376,200 marketing/creative jobs posted in the U.S. in 2025** (Robert Half), with **65% of marketing leaders planning to expand permanent headcount** in H1 2026. TrueUp shows the combined category at **11,156 open roles, up 26.9% YoY**. But the distribution within marketing has shifted dramatically.

**Product marketing remains strong**: 24,800 PMM positions posted in 2025, reflecting the growing overlap between marketing, product strategy, and customer experience. PMMs define positioning, messaging, and go-to-market strategy — work that requires judgment AI cannot easily replicate. **Performance marketing is similarly robust**, with 64,900 digital marketing role postings and marketing automation manager positions growing 10% year-over-year.

**Content execution roles are under serious pressure.** Bloomberry's analysis of 180 million job postings found writers and copywriters down **28%**, PR specialists down **21%**. Generative AI can now produce brand-voice content, adapt it across channels, and scale production in ways that directly displace junior content creators. The Taligence U.S. Marketing Jobs Report (analyzing 240,000+ listings) identified a clear "K-shaped" split: "New grads are fighting for oxygen while the middle — those player-coach types who can still write copy and read a P&L — are the ones getting the calls back." Marketing professionals with AI skills command a **20–30% salary premium**, and in marketing/sales specifically, applied AI skills trigger average pay bumps of approximately **43%**.

## Recruiter demand is flashing the strongest bullish signal in the data

Lenny calls this finding the most important leading indicator in the March 2026 report: **open recruiter roles are "almost back to 2022 peak levels."** This role got hit hardest during the post-2022 contraction and has recovered the fastest. The logic is structural: "By definition, recruiting headcount expands and contracts with hiring demand, so it's likely a leading indication that we're tracking toward sustained highs in hiring demand in tech." MSH Recruitment data shows demand for talent acquisition specialists **increased 87% in the last year**. Employer branding budgets have increased **107%** over the past five years.

AI is transforming recruiting but not replacing it. Korn Ferry reports **84% of talent leaders will use AI in 2026**, and 52% plan to add autonomous AI agents to their recruiting teams. But only **26% of applicants trust AI to evaluate them fairly** (Gartner), creating enduring demand for human oversight. The EU AI Act classifies HR use of AI as "high-risk," with full compliance required by **August 2, 2026** — creating additional demand for human recruiters who understand compliance requirements.

## Data roles are strong but increasingly blurred with AI/ML engineering

Data Science & Machine Learning is TrueUp's fastest-growing tracked category: **9,075 open roles, up 90.1% year-over-year**. Data engineering specifically is booming — roughly **150,000 data engineering professionals in North America**, with **20,000+ hired last year** representing ~23% growth. The World Economic Forum's 2025 Future of Jobs Report placed data roles in **five of the top fifteen fastest-growing jobs** globally.

Traditional data science is not disappearing but is converging with AI/ML engineering. LLM engineering now appears in **12% of data science job postings** (up from 3%), and agentic AI skills have gone from 0% to 8% in just one year. The BLS projects **34% growth for data scientists by 2034**. Average data engineer salary sits at approximately **$135,654**, but specialized roles command much more: computer vision engineers earn $174K–$219K, and AI engineers $151K–$213K.

## Scrum master is the only role in structural decline

Lenny's Live Data Technologies analysis was unambiguous: **scrum master is the only role with net-negative hiring** — more people being let go each month than hired. TrueUp's March 2026 data shows just **60 open scrum master listings at tech companies globally, down 38.8% year-over-year**. That is not a typo — sixty positions across 9,000+ tracked companies.

The qualitative evidence is equally stark. Certified Scrum Master class enrollment has collapsed from **49% of students in 2020 to less than 5% in 2024**. One prominent trainer stopped offering public CSM classes entirely. Companies are systematically eliminating the Scrum Master and Agile Coach job titles. Lenny theorized this partly explains the growth in product owner roles (up 34.9% YoY) — "people who used to be called scrum masters are now called product owners." The Perplexity CEO captured the zeitgeist: "If I see the terms 'Agile expert' or 'scrum master' in the resume, it's probably not going to be a great fit." AI tools like Geekbot, Standuply, and Spinach.io now automate standups, sprint metrics, and retrospective summaries — the administrative functions that once justified dedicated scrum master headcount.

## European hiring trails the U.S. but shows distinct patterns

European tech hiring stabilized at a **29% rate in 2025** (Ravio), flat versus 2024 but down from 34% in 2023. Tech job postings declined steeply across major markets: **Switzerland –46%, UK –41%, France –39%, Germany –30%** from pre-pandemic baselines. The overall recovery lags the U.S., but AI-specific hiring is accelerating: GenAI job postings surged **+204% in Ireland, +120% in UK, +109% in Germany, and +91% in France** through early 2025.

The most notable divergence is in the **commercial/GTM function**, which is the **only function with increasing hiring rates** in European tech at **34.7%** (up 5%) — the highest rate across all functions. This suggests European companies are prioritizing revenue generation while holding engineering and product headcount steady. The salary gap between EU and U.S. remains significant: European mid-level software developers earn **$57,000–$75,000** versus **$110,000–$134,000** in the U.S. Total employment costs in London run **48% less** than Seattle. AI/ML roles command a **10–20% salary uplift** over general engineering across most European markets. The EU AI Act creates unique compliance requirements, with HR use of AI classified as high-risk and full compliance required by August 2026 — generating demand for AI governance roles that don't yet exist at scale in the U.S.

London, Paris, Berlin, Amsterdam, Zurich, and Stockholm remain the six premier European tech hubs. **81% of European AI founders** now build from Europe (up from 74% in 2016), though 18% of experienced founders headquarter in the U.S. — up from 10%. Berlin and Austin both entered Lenny's top 10 PM cities globally in 2025.

## Conclusion: the new shape of a startup team

The data across all ten role categories points to a structural transformation, not a cyclical recovery. Five patterns define the 2026 hiring landscape that anyone building a European AI job exposure tool should internalize.

**First, the AI premium is real and widening.** AI/ML roles command 10–56% salary premiums, are growing 90%+ year-over-year, and are concentrated in a small number of geographies. Any exposure model must weight AI fluency as the single strongest positive signal for job security and compensation growth across every function.

**Second, seniority is the new floor.** Entry-level hiring has collapsed 73% across European tech. Companies want "player-coaches" — senior ICs who can leverage AI tools to do what previously required teams. The exposure risk for junior roles is dramatically higher than for senior equivalents across PM, engineering, design, marketing, and operations.

**Third, the "judgment vs. execution" divide cuts through every role.** Roles centered on strategic judgment (Growth PM, Product Marketing, Sales Engineering, RevOps, Senior Design) are thriving. Roles centered on production execution (junior content writer, SDR doing cold email, scrum master running standups, entry-level designer producing wireframes) face compression or elimination.

**Fourth, recruiter demand as leading indicator suggests the recovery has further to run.** With recruiter openings nearly back to 2022 peaks, the forward signal points to sustained or increasing hiring demand through 2026.

**Fifth, Europe has its own trajectory.** Slower overall recovery, a distinctive commercial/GTM hiring emphasis, significant salary gaps versus the U.S., and the EU AI Act creating both compliance burden and new role categories. An effective European exposure tool must account for these structural differences rather than simply applying U.S. data with a discount factor.
