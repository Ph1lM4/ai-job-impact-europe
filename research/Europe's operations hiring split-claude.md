# Europe's operations hiring split: strategic roles rise while routine work collapses

**European tech operations hiring stands at 27%, down 20% year-over-year, but this headline masks a dramatic internal fracture.** Entry-level operations roles have collapsed by 73.4% as AI automates routine work, while compliance-driven strategic operations hiring is surging — creating a labor market divergence unique to Europe. The continent's regulatory stack (NIS2, EU AI Act, DORA, EAA) is generating hundreds of thousands of new compliance operations roles with no US equivalent, even as traditional BizOps, admin-ops, and junior finance roles disappear. This report presents verified data from Ravio, ENISA, CLOC, Gartner, Robert Half, and other primary sources to map this bifurcation across sub-roles, countries, and salary bands.

---

## The 27% headline hides a 73% junior hiring collapse

Ravio's 2026 Compensation Trends Report — drawing on HRIS-integrated data from **1,400+ European tech companies and 350,000+ employees** — confirms the operations hiring rate at **27% in 2025, down 20% YoY** from approximately 33.75% in 2024. Despite this decline, operations maintains the **second-highest hiring rate** of any function after Commercial (34.7%). The overall European tech hiring rate held steady at 29%, meaning operations is declining faster than the market.

The severity concentrates at the bottom of the ladder. Ravio's 2025 Tech Job Market Report documents a **73.4% decline in P1/P2 (entry-level) hiring** across all tech functions in the past year, versus only a **7% decrease across all levels**. Operations, marketing, people, and administration saw "even steeper drops than the 73.4% overall entry-level average." Half of surveyed Reward leaders explicitly cited AI automation as the reason for deprioritizing junior roles, with respondents stating "they can be automated" and "AI can substitute." Meanwhile, **AI/ML hiring grew 88% YoY**, and AI-related job titles expanded **578%** (from 0.32% to 2.17% of all roles).

The operations function is simultaneously bleeding talent at all levels: **attrition hit 21.3%** — the highest of any function versus the 17.4% average. Only **14% of operations employees received salary increases** (versus 23% overall), and **promotion rates sit at a mere 3.4%**. Ravio describes "considerable Operations hiring, perhaps reflecting high replacement demand" rather than growth, and explicitly identifies "a bifurcation between routine tasks being automated and strategic work becoming more important."

Country-level Ravio data reveals stark divergences in hiring rates:

| Country | 2025 hiring rate | YoY change |
|---------|-----------------|------------|
| UK | 32% | −21% |
| Germany | 30% | +3% |
| Spain | 26% | −27% |
| Netherlands | 24% | −13% |
| France | 22% | −28% |
| Sweden | 17% | −34% |

**Germany is the only European market with positive hiring growth** (+3%), likely driven by its outsized exposure to NIS2 compliance (Germany alone needs an estimated **106,000 additional cybersecurity professionals by 2026**, per recruitment firm VargheseJackson). Sweden's 34% decline is the steepest in Europe. LinkedIn's EMEA Labour Market Outlook corroborates broad weakness: the technology sector hiring index (versus a 2016 baseline of 1.0) stands at **0.75 in the UK, 0.74 in Germany, and 0.59 in France** — meaning tech hiring is 25-41% below decade-ago levels. Indeed data shows European tech job postings falling more steeply than any other global region: Switzerland −46%, UK −41%, France −39%, Germany −30%.

## Europe's regulatory stack creates operations demand the US doesn't have

The defining feature of the European operations labor market is compliance-driven hiring generated by a regulatory "stack" that has no US equivalent. Multiple overlapping regulations compound into massive workforce demand — a European bank simultaneously faces DORA + NIS2 + GDPR + AI Act, while a European tech company faces AI Act + NIS2 + DSA/DMA + EAA + GDPR + the Cyber Resilience Act. This structural demand diverges sharply from the US market, where Section 230 provides broad platform immunity, no cross-sector cybersecurity mandate exists, and there is no federal AI law.

**NIS2 Directive** (effective October 2024) expanded mandatory cybersecurity compliance to **160,000+ entities** across the EU. ENISA's NIS Investments 2025 Report (1,080 respondents) found **70% of organizations cite regulatory compliance as their primary cybersecurity investment driver**, while **76% face difficulties attracting cybersecurity professionals** and **71% struggle to retain them**. The ISC2 2024 Workforce Study documents a European cybersecurity workforce gap of **424,000 skilled individuals** (up 9.8% YoY), with ENISA reporting **~299,000 specific open positions**. New roles being created include CISOs, incident response handlers, business continuity planners, supply chain risk managers, and cybersecurity auditors.

**DORA** (effective January 2025) applies prescriptive ICT resilience requirements to **22,000+ financial entities** across the EU, plus critical ICT service providers. McKinsey found **40% of surveyed companies dedicate 7+ full-time employees to DORA register management alone**. The ESAs' dry run exercise saw **only 6.5% of registers pass**, and **43% of organizations** admitted they wouldn't be fully compliant for at least three months post-deadline — signaling sustained hiring demand. EY/IIF data shows **75% of global Chief Risk Officers** now cite cybersecurity as their foremost concern.

**The EU AI Act** (high-risk obligations effective August 2026) is spawning an entirely new job family. The European AI governance market is projected to reach **$312.4 million by 2030** at a **33% CAGR**. Forrester predicts **60% of Fortune 100 companies will appoint a head of AI governance by end of 2026**. Emerging roles include AI Compliance Officers, AI Ethics Officers, AI Auditors, AI Risk Managers, and AI Governance Administrators — positions commanding **£95,000–£225,000** in the UK market.

**The European Accessibility Act** (effective June 2025) covers e-commerce, banking, transport, and digital services across all 27 member states, with penalties up to **€100,000 or 4% of annual revenue**. Bird & Bird found **75% of the most visited online shops in Germany** and **72% of household-name brands in Ireland** fail accessibility standards, indicating substantial remediation hiring ahead.

The European cybersecurity market overall stands at approximately **€67 billion in 2025**, projected to reach **€172 billion by 2033** at **12.4% CAGR**. But cybersecurity FTEs now represent only **10.6% of IT FTEs** — the lowest ever — indicating a parallel shift toward outsourcing and technology solutions even as total compliance spending surges.

## RevOps surges globally but Europe lags the US by years

Revenue Operations has become one of the fastest-growing functions globally, but European adoption trails North America significantly. Gartner's original 2021 prediction that **75% of the highest-growth companies would deploy a RevOps model by 2025** has been extended to 2026, with adoption broadly on track: **79% of organizations entering 2025 have a formal RevOps function** (Skaled data), and **48% of all companies now have a dedicated RevOps team** — a **15% jump from the prior year** (B2B Mix). The VP of Revenue Operations title grew **300% over the past 18 months**, and "RevOps" is now the **fifth fastest-growing search on LinkedIn**.

European adoption lags measurably. The RevOps Co-op notes: "The term RevOps has existed in the US SaaS space for more than a decade, but in Europe and particularly in France, it's only in the last few years the title has begun to be defined." European companies frequently use "Sales Operations" or "Commercial Operations" titles for functionally equivalent roles, at lower pay bands. The UK is the most mature European RevOps market, with Future Market Insights estimating an incremental opportunity of **$3.42 billion** during the forecast period. The global RevOps platform market is growing at **17.3% CAGR**, projected to reach **$18.06 billion by 2033**.

Adoption rates break sharply by company size: enterprise companies show **84% adoption**, midmarket **52%**, and small businesses just **21%** (though accelerating at 30% per year). BCG data shows top B2B tech companies with RevOps achieve **10-20% increases in sales productivity** and **30% reduction in GTM expenses**. PeerSignal's analysis of 2,500 B2B SaaS companies establishes a benchmark of **12:1 sales reps to RevOps personnel**.

## Chief of Staff grows in tech while Finance Ops and Legal Ops diverge

**Chief of Staff roles** are expanding, particularly in tech (which accounts for **25.7% of all CoS positions globally**). Bain Capital Ventures observes "more Chief of Staff open roles than ever before" across their portfolio companies. However, the role remains heavily US-concentrated — LinkedIn lists **10,000+ CoS jobs in the US** versus roughly **18 remote CoS roles across Europe** on specialized boards. London, Berlin, and Amsterdam are the primary European CoS hubs. The Chief of Staff Network on LinkedIn has **21,540+ followers**. Compensation scales significantly by stage: early-stage CoS roles pay £62,000–£82,000 in London, while late-stage/pre-IPO positions reach £120,000–£200,000. European CoS salaries run **30-45% below US equivalents** (US average: $167,954 base, per the Chief of Staff Network's 2025 report).

**Finance operations** presents a mixed picture. Robert Half's 2026 UK Salary Guide reports **45% of businesses plan to grow finance teams**, focusing on roles that blend financial expertise with AI capabilities. Hays found **69% of UK accounting/finance employers plan to hire**, with **92% experiencing skill shortages**. Yet the Deloitte European CFO Survey (1,738 CFOs across 20 countries) shows only **~30% plan to hire more workers**, with 27% expecting cuts. The CSRD Omnibus I package (December 2025) significantly reduced regulatory burden, exempting **~80% of companies initially in scope** and reducing mandatory data points by 61%. However, Pillar Two global minimum tax implementation (now in **22 of 27 EU member states**) continues to drive finance operations complexity. AI adoption in finance is accelerating rapidly: **72% of finance leaders now use AI tools** (up from 34% the prior year, per Protiviti), and **84% of finance leaders personally use generative AI** (Datarails). The ECB's March 2026 analysis of 5,300 euro area firms found that firms investing in AI are **more likely to be hiring, not less** — suggesting AI is currently complementing rather than replacing finance operations roles.

**Legal operations** is experiencing structural transformation. CLOC's 2025 State of the Industry Report (186 organizations, 14 countries) found **83% of legal departments expect demand to increase**, with **63% identifying workload/bandwidth as their top challenge**. **87% of respondents have legal ops as a full-time role.** Median legal ops team sizes range from **2 professionals** at companies under $3B revenue to **8 professionals** at companies over $40B. The 2026 CLOC report identifies a "structural productivity gap": **only 32% expect attorney headcount growth**, while demand rises in complex areas — making legal operations the strategic lever to close this gap. A strong **insourcing trend** is underway: only **37% expect outside counsel spend increases**, down sharply from **58% the prior year**, with nearly 40% of departments shifting more responsibility to internal staff. AI adoption in legal departments doubled from 18% to **30%** between 2023 and 2025, and **85% of legal departments now have dedicated AI oversight**. Notably, **65% of law firms are hiring legal tech experts over traditional attorneys** (JD Journal), while TRU Staffing Partners predicts "turnover will drive most new opportunities in legal operations as opposed to newly created jobs" — suggesting headcount stabilization rather than growth.

## Conclusion: two operations labor markets are emerging in Europe

The European operations hiring landscape is not contracting uniformly — it is splitting into two distinct markets that will likely accelerate apart through 2026-2027.

**Routine operations** (junior BizOps, administrative operations, manual data-processing roles, basic financial operations) is experiencing genuine structural elimination. The 73.4% entry-level hiring collapse is not cyclical — it reflects permanent AI substitution of tasks that defined P1/P2 operations roles. Operations attrition at 21.3% (the highest of any function) combined with the lowest raise eligibility (14%) and promotion rates (3.4%) confirms these roles are being deprioritized, not temporarily frozen. The fact that overall operations hiring remains at 27% despite this junior collapse means the function is being restaffed at higher levels with different skill profiles.

**Strategic operations** — compliance operations, AI governance, RevOps, senior finance operations, legal operations technology — is in structural growth driven by forces unique to Europe. The regulatory stack (NIS2 affecting 160,000 entities, DORA covering 22,000 financial institutions, the EU AI Act creating an entirely new governance discipline, EAA mandating digital accessibility across 27 member states) is generating **hundreds of thousands of compliance-adjacent operations roles** that simply do not exist in the US market. Europe's cybersecurity workforce gap alone stands at 424,000. Germany's anomalous positive hiring growth (+3% versus declines of 13-34% elsewhere) is likely explained by its outsized compliance exposure.

Three trends deserve close monitoring for DACH-focused intelligence. First, **Germany's operations labor market is uniquely resilient** — the only European market with positive hiring growth, driven by compliance demand and its large industrial base requiring operational resilience. Second, **RevOps adoption in Europe remains 3-5 years behind the US**, representing both a gap and an opportunity as European SaaS companies mature. Third, **the legal operations insourcing trend** (outside counsel spend expectations dropping from 58% to 37% in one year) combined with AI adoption doubling to 30% suggests legal ops is transforming from a cost center into a technology-enabled strategic function. The European operations professional who survives this bifurcation will be one who combines regulatory literacy, AI fluency, and cross-functional strategic capability — the antithesis of the routine operations roles now being eliminated.
